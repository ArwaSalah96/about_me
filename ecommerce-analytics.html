DATA SOURCE & BUSINESS CONTEXT

Understanding the Olist dataset.

The project uses the Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist, downloaded from Kaggle. It contains approximately 100,000 orders made between 2016 and 2018 through Brazilian online marketplaces.

The dataset consists of nine related CSV files covering different parts of the e-commerce process. These include customers, orders, order items, products, sellers, payments, reviews, geographical information, and product category translations.

Olist connects small businesses with online marketplaces and supports their sales and order fulfillment processes. This makes the dataset useful for exploring the relationships between commercial performance, customers, sellers, logistics, and customer satisfaction.

The original dataset contains real commercial data that has been anonymized by Olist. The analysis in this project is based on the publicly available dataset, not on private company data.

Dataset source: Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist – Kaggle

SQL SERVER · DATA EXPLORATION & PREPARATION

Working with the data before visualization.

The analysis started in SQL Server, where I explored the source tables, examined their structure, and worked on combining and preparing the data for further analysis.

Since the dataset was distributed across multiple related tables, an important part of the work involved understanding the relationships between orders, customers, products, sellers, payments, reviews, and geographical information.

Relational Data Exploration

Explored the available tables and their columns, reviewed the underlying structure, and used joins to combine information from different business entities.

Joins & Aggregations

Combined orders with order items, products, customers, sellers, and reviews. Aggregated order-level information such as item counts, product counts, seller counts, item totals, and shipping costs.

Payment Analysis

Used SQL PIVOT to transform payment types into separate columns and aggregated payment values by order to support comparisons between payments and order totals.

Views & Summary Tables

Created summarized order views and used Common Table Expressions to organize and investigate aggregated results and check the consistency of order-level summaries.

These queries were written in SQL Server and were used to explore, transform, summarize, and validate the source data before reporting.

DATA CLEANING & VALIDATION

Improving data reliability.

A significant part of the project involved investigating data quality issues, especially within the geographical information. The geolocation table contained multiple coordinate records for the same ZIP code prefix, and the data needed additional checks before being used for geographical reporting.

Geographical Coordinate Validation

Checked latitude and longitude values against the geographical boundaries of Brazil to identify coordinates that were outside the expected country boundaries.

Coordinate Cleaning

Created cleaned latitude and longitude fields, using valid coordinates from matching ZIP code prefixes where possible, while leaving unmatched values as NULL.

ZIP Code Consolidation

Used grouping and ranking logic with ROW_NUMBER() to select the most frequent geographical record for each ZIP code prefix and build a more consistent location reference table.

City & State Validation

Compared geographical city and state information with an additional Brazilian cities reference dataset, investigated mismatches, and standardized city names to improve matching.

Data Reconciliation

Compared values from related datasets and investigated differences between order totals, payment amounts, and geographical attributes.

Missing Delivery Information

Investigated missing customer delivery dates and explored an estimated-date approach based on the average time between carrier and customer delivery dates.

Estimated delivery dates derived from missing values should be distinguished from observed delivery dates when interpreting delivery performance. The estimation approach is a data preparation assumption, not a record of an actual delivery event.

POWER BI · REPORT DESIGN

Three perspectives on e-commerce performance.

01 · Sales & Orders

The first page provides an overview of overall e-commerce activity and sales performance.

  • Total sales and seller count.
  • Order volume trends over time.
  • Monthly comparison between order totals and shipping totals.
  • Order delivery status distribution.
  • Customer counts by year.

02 · Location Analysis

The second page explores the geographical distribution of customers and sellers across Brazil.

  • Number of seller cities and sellers.

  • Number of orders by seller city.

03 · Customer Reviews

The third page focuses on customer satisfaction and review patterns.

  • Total reviews.
  • Positive and negative review counts.
  • Top 20 products with negative reviews.
  • Top 20 products with positive reviews.
  • Seller cities with the highest number of negative reviews.
  • Seller cities with the highest number of positive reviews.

Interactive Analysis

The report brings the different analytical views together in Power BI, making it easier to explore sales, geographical distribution, and customer feedback through visual summaries and detailed comparisons.

BUSINESS VALUE

Who can benefit from this analysis?

By connecting sales, customer, seller, logistics, and review data, the dashboard provides different stakeholders with a more complete view of e-commerce operations. Each analytical page addresses a different business question.

Business Owners & Management

Can monitor sales performance, order activity, customer distribution, and product demand to better understand overall business performance and identify areas that may require attention.

Sales & Commercial Teams

Can explore sales trends, compare customer cities, identify high-performing products, and understand where orders and sales are concentrated.

Logistics & Operations Teams

Can review delivery statuses, shipping costs, and geographical patterns to investigate delivery-related performance and understand differences across locations.

Customer Experience Teams

Can analyze positive and negative reviews, identify products associated with customer dissatisfaction, and investigate geographical patterns in customer feedback.

Product & Marketplace Teams

Can explore product demand, review patterns, and seller activity to support product assortment discussions and marketplace performance monitoring.

Data & BI Teams

Can use the consolidated reporting approach to make complex relational data easier to explore, communicate, and reuse for business analysis.

This project demonstrates the complete analytical journey from raw relational data to an interactive business intelligence solution.

The project highlights how combining SQL and data visualization can help transform large, disconnected datasets into structured information that supports better business understanding and more informed decisions.
